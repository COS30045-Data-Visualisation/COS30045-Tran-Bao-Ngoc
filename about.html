About Us

This website looks at how much electricity household appliances use in Australia. It was built for COS30045 Data Visualisation at Swinburne Vietnam.

The audience

The television story is written for Australian households and everyday consumers who are thinking about buying or replacing a TV. It uses familiar units, screen size in inches and energy use in kWh per year, and avoids technical statistics.

The question

How does television screen size relate to annual energy consumption? More specifically, do larger TVs generally use more electricity each year, and is the difference big enough to matter when choosing a new TV?

About the data

The dataset describes televisions and their energy performance. The analysis uses these variables:

VariableMeaning
Screen size (inches)Size of the TV screen, rounded for grouped analysis
Labelled energy consumption (kWh/year)Labelled annual electricity use of the TV
Screen size classificationSmall, Medium or Large group created for this analysis

The data was processed in KNIME. A Rule Engine placed each TV into a Small, Medium or Large group, and a GroupBy step calculated the average annual energy consumption of each group. The data describes products, not people, so it raises no significant individual privacy concerns.

Accuracy and limitations

The analysis shows an association between screen size and annual energy consumption. It does not prove that screen size alone causes higher energy use.

TVs of similar size can use different amounts of electricity, so other factors such as display technology, model design and energy efficiency probably matter too. The Small, Medium and Large groups were created for this analysis and depend on the thresholds chosen in KNIME. The results describe the TVs in this dataset and should not be read as describing every TV on sale in Australia.

Ethics

The charts were designed to show the overall pattern without exaggerating it. The story does not say that large TVs are bad or that households should avoid them. It gives information to weigh alongside viewing needs, energy efficiency and other features.
